# Who could the unidentified commander be?

Assessment dated 14 September 2026. **La Ferté-Senneterre is the leading historical
candidate, with Turenne second. Neither identification is cryptographically
established.** The code group `"40` at original position 133 remains unassigned.

## What the wording suggests

The current reconstruction says:

> …[X], par la marche qu’il a ordre de faire, leur donnera lieu de craindre pour Rocroi…

X has orders to march in a way that makes the enemy fear for Rocroi. Castelnau
should make them believe his own march will join X, if the tentative ending
*joindre* is correct. This suggests coordination between two French forces,
with their apparent destination intended to mislead the enemy.

The masculine singular pronoun supports a male referent but establishes no rank.
The single code group could represent a full name and title, so a candidate’s
name length provides no useful constraint. These deductions depend on the present
partial reading, not on an independently recovered codebook.

## Candidate assessment

**1. Marshal de La Ferté-Senneterre — strongest current historical fit.** A history
of the 1657 campaign places La Ferté opposing Condé in the Entre-Sambre-et-Meuse
region before Condé hurried away to relieve Cambrai in late May. That makes him
a plausible commander for the eastern movement described in the letter. This is
later-May context, not a record of his orders on 12 May.
[Campaign history, published 1896](https://www.persee.fr/doc/marb_0770-8459_1896_num_54_1_2426)

A more specific lead comes from Ria Van Praet’s study of *La Gazette*. Its summary
of a report dated **25 June 1657**, from the camp near Montmédy, describes La Ferté
taking the road towards Rocroi to make the enemy believe he intended to attack
the town. The study identifies Gazette no. 79, p. 633. This resembles the tactic
in the letter, but the report is six weeks later. Only the study’s summary has
been consulted; the original Gazette passage and the date of the movement still
need checking. It is supporting context, not confirmation of `"40`.
[Van Praet, study p. 144](https://libstore.ugent.be/fulltxt/RUG01/001/414/650/RUG01-001414650_2010_0001_AC.pdf#page=144)

**2. Turenne — credible alternative.** An account of the opening campaign describes
Turenne moving towards the maritime front before turning suddenly against Cambrai.
Coordinating Castelnau’s movement would fit that broader strategy of deception.
However, this account gives a less direct connection between his particular march
and a threat to Rocroi. This relative ranking is an inference from the sources.
[Duc d’Aumale, campaign account, section VIII](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/La_Lutte_entre_Turenne_et_Cond%C3%A9_%281654-1657%29/02)

**Condé is much less plausible under the present reading.** He was operating with
the Spanish side, whereas X’s march appears intended to frighten that side for
Rocroi. The same campaign account describes Condé relieving Cambrai against
Turenne. A subordinate French commander remains possible; the shortlist is not
exhaustive. [Campaign account](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/La_Lutte_entre_Turenne_et_Cond%C3%A9_%281654-1657%29/02)

## Evidence that could decide the question

The most useful next search is La Ferté’s orders and correspondence from roughly
**8–15 May 1657**, followed by comparison with Turenne’s movements and the royal
instructions accompanying this letter. An instruction linking a threat to Rocroi
with Castelnau’s march would materially strengthen the identification. A matching
key or parallel plaintext could establish the actual expansion of `"40`.

Until then, the readable solution retains **[B : sujet non identifié]**, and the
working key and literal decoder output retain the unresolved code group.
